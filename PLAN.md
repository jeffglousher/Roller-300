Need actual low-setting breakaway/running torque tolerance, temperature/wear drift, adjustment retention, permitted slip speed and heat/duty information. If installed cost or low-torque separation is unsuitable, the sole fallback is a hybrid printed carrier with purchased metal plates, friction lining and metal springs. Its wear kit has not been selected or calibrated. Neither purchased RC wear parts nor printed carriers make it a qualified limiter. Do not order multiple clutches until the one-side torque window exists.

At the wheel shaft require normal-operation peak plus justified margin < lowest measured breakaway, and highest breakaway/transient < weakest protected permissible torque. Reflect transmission losses/direction separately; stall torque alone establishes neither limit. With radius0.123 m,1 N·m corresponds to8.13 N at ONE wheel (16.26 N for two, before traction effects). This is not a safe-force claim or selected setting. Actual mass, tire deformation and rounded floor profile determine normal crossing demands; revise mass/ratio/wheel/material if the protective band does not exist.

Clutch heat isT_slip×relative_rad/s. At the calculated30.5 rpm output,1 N·m slip would generate3.19 W; even a short event needs a bounded duration/temperature basis. No sustained-slip rating, time allowance or current cutoff is assumed. Current can remain inconspicuous during slip. A downstream wheel sensor alone measures wheel rotation, not both clutch-member speeds; it can detect commanded-no-motion only with justified logic, and cannot identify moving-wheel slip without upstream feedback. Electrical/software implementation remains deferred.

Fault coverage: wheel obstruction/back-driving crosses the limiter; a jammed belt/pulley upstream can load the servo before the output limiter; reversal shock is cushioned but still needs load qualification; seized clutch removes mechanical torque limiting. Hold-to-run and accessible actuator-power disconnect are required for supervised powered tests. Before powered testing, define numeric current, surface/internal temperature, torque and slip-duration stops from actual hardware evidence and independent calibration. No such numerical powered-test envelope has yet been justified, so powered release remains held. Before broader driving, implement fault detection/stop of both sides and deliberate rearm, or prove worst permitted thermal duty. Loss of commands, brownout, reboot, power restoration and Pi failure must leave drive off until deliberate rearm; this behavior is required, not currently implemented. A clutch is not a parking brake.

Guard belt meshes, adapters, shaft ends and wheel/body gaps. The5 mm nominal wheel gap is not a finger-clearance approval. The shown removable cover is an inspection proposal; its shaft passages and overlap with the wheel shield need closure before operation. Wheel runout, bearing play, tire bulge and frame flex must fit within the width/gap tolerance budget. Cameras and charging contacts must not project beyond the round protective belly.

Confirm printed-part connectivity and interference with complete fastener/tool envelopes. Release frame/pulleys/guard only after those checks. A valid solid is not proof of an assembled mechanism.
3. Independently calibrate the clutch in a supported fixture with measured force at a known lever arm, in both directions, repeated breakaway and running slip; retain adjustment witness marks. No fingers near motion and no servo overstress as a measurement method. Thermal cycling begins only after numeric limits are justified.
4. Inspect gearboxes, belt/pulleys, friction surfaces, caps, shafts and printed joints afterward. Retain that qualified end section, print its mirrored mate after any corrections, bolt them to the matching round body, and perform raised-wheel failsafe checks followed by supervised ground/floor-transition tests.
